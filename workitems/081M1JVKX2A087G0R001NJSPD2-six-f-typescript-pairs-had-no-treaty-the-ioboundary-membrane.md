# Six F#/TypeScript pairs had no treaty — the `IoBoundary` membrane is now pinned

## The sweep

535 F# modules against 1411 TypeScript ones. **68 concepts are implemented in BOTH languages.** 62
are pinned by a treaty or golden vectors. **Six were not:**

```
ErasureCharge · IndexedZSet · IoBoundary · RecoverableSpine · SnapshotStore · SpecializationCache
```

An unpinned pair is two implementations of one idea with **nothing checking they agree** — the exact
divergence the four-oracle byte-lock discipline exists to price.

## Why `IoBoundary` first

It is the §13 noninterference membrane: it decides what leaves the interior. If the two sides
disagreed, one would leak a fact the other retracted.

And it had two concrete divergence risks, not just an abstract one:

1. **F# `IoBoundary.fuse` DELEGATES** to `FusionReconstruction.fuse`; **TypeScript REIMPLEMENTS**
   the positive-support filter inline (`entry.w > 0`). They agree today — I read both — and *"agree
   today"* is a statement with a shelf life.
2. **Order.** F# takes its ascending order from `'K : comparison`, which is **ordinal** for strings;
   TypeScript takes an explicit `compare`. A `localeCompare` anywhere would reorder mixed-case keys.
   Ordinally `[B;a;A;b]` fuses to `[A;B;a;b]`; under a locale collation it gives `[a;A;b;B]`.

## The treaty

59 vectors across five shapes — `Fuse`, `ComposeThenFuse`, `ComposeAllThenFuse`, `Contains`,
`EmitRetractThenFuse` — generated by the TypeScript and replayed through the F#, the same mechanism
as `HatTreaty` and `WorkflowEngine`. The string vectors are deliberately case-mixed so the collation
difference cannot hide, and a test asserts that vector still **exists** so nobody quietly deletes the
one case that discriminates.

**Result: the two implementations already agreed.** 7/7 F# tests green on the first run. The treaty
does not fix a divergence; it converts *"they agree today"* into a statement that stays true.

## The falsifier: does the treaty CATCH a divergence?

A treaty that passes on two agreeing implementations proves only that they agree. So each mutant
below breaks **one side**, regenerates the transcript from it, and replays through the other:

**6/6 killed** — `fuse` dropping singly-supported keys, `fuse` leaking retracted keys, a
case-insensitive collation, `compose` dropping its right operand, `retract` emitting a positive
weight, and `count` reporting the wrong size.

## Two mutants I wrote that were UNOBSERVABLE, and why

Both survived at first, and neither is a gap in the treaty:

- **`w >= 0` instead of `w > 0`** — unreachable. `ZSet.ofEntries` ends with
  `.filter(entry => entry.w !== 0)` and `union` cancels to `[]`, so a zero-weight entry can never
  reach `fuse`. Verified by probe, not assumed: `ofEntries([a:0, b:1, c:-1])` → `[b:1, c:-1]`.
- **swapping `union`'s operands** — a no-op, because per-key sum is commutative.

Replaced with mutations that are actually observable. Recording this because "a surviving mutant"
and "an untested property" are different things, and treating the first as the second would have
sent me writing a vector for a state the type system forbids.

## Still open

Five unpinned pairs remain: `ErasureCharge`, `IndexedZSet`, `RecoverableSpine`, `SnapshotStore`,
`SpecializationCache`. `IndexedZSet` is the largest surface (380 F# / 329 TS lines) and the DBSP
core; `SnapshotStore` and `RecoverableSpine` are durability, where a snapshot written by one side and
read by the other is the failure that matters.
