> "yes we build tamper resistance into our itron meters, i forget this is not common
> sense to others"

## The prior he brings

**Revenue-grade metering is the one discipline where tamper-evidence is MANDATORY** —
legally, economically, adversarially. Sealed enclosures, tamper flags in the event log,
magnetic-field detection, reverse-flow detection, audit trails surviving removal from
the wall. Not thoroughness: someone always tries, the incentive is money, and a
regulator checks. Centuries of adversarial metrology with a real economy attached.

**Software metrics are the opposite** — advisory, self-reported, unsealed, unaudited.
Coverage, story points, contribution graphs: honor-system numbers nobody would accept
on a utility bill.

## The reframe worth using (his thesis, stated in his own expertise)

> **Revenue-grade metrology applied to AI contribution, in a domain that currently runs
> on honor-system counters.**

Better than the TSMC analogy: more concrete, explains why *checkability* keeps
surfacing as the load-bearing property in every branch
([[tsmc-precision-bet-honest-meter-is-the-thesis]]), and it is **his own expertise**
rather than a borrowed industry analogy.

## The blind spot, and it is actionable

What is table stakes in metering is a **novel claim** in software. So the parts he
finds too obvious to mention are often **the parts carrying the argument**.

Pairs with the note that he can explain his shapes in common vernacular and that this
is the real ownership test: *"we build tamper resistance into meters because people
steal power"* lands instantly; *"content-addressed hash-chained ledger"* does not — and
they are the same sentence. Prompt him to say the vernacular version when the audience
lacks the prior.

## Boundary

The *discipline* of tamper-resistant metering is industry-wide and centuries old — free
to use and to teach. Itron's specific implementations are not
([[itron-hub-patent-boundary-p2p-is-the-upgrade]], and the clean-room rule: the
expertise is usable, the code is not).
