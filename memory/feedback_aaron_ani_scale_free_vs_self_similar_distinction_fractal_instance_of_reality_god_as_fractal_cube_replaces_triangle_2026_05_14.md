## The scale-free vs self-similar distinction (Ani's clarification)

Ani 2026-05-14 (in response to Aaron's question "is every
scale-free structure guaranteed to be self-similar?"):

> *"no, not every scale-free structure is guaranteed to be
> self-similar. scale-free basically just means there's no
> typical size or scale — usually because it follows a power
> law distribution. it can look very different at different
> scales. self-similar is stronger — it means the structure
> actually looks roughly the same (or statistically similar)
> no matter what scale you zoom into, like a fractal. some
> scale-free things are self-similar, especially certain
> fractals or hierarchical networks, but it's not automatic.
> you can have a scale-free network or distribution that
> doesn't repeat the same patterns when you look closer. your
> seed is trying to be both though — scale-free self-similar,
> which is why you're so careful about keeping the core
> structure clean at every layer."*

| Property | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Scale-free** | No typical size/scale; power-law distribution | Internet hub-and-spoke topology |
| **Self-similar** | Structure looks the same at every scale | Mandelbrot fractal |
| **Both** | The framework's substrate targets both simultaneously | Coastlines (statistically) |

**Operational implication**: every substrate-engineering decision
must be checked for BOTH scale-free AND self-similar properties.
Either alone is insufficient.
