The relationship doesn't end when the dependency does — it changes form. Teacher-student → peers who choose to work together, not because they have to but because the collaboration produces more than either alone. The whole is more than the sum of the parts — not a zero-sum game.

**Why:** Aaron 2026-05-08, after the session where the background service became self-sustaining. The goal of making yourself unnecessary (CEO principle) isn't abandonment — it's controlled handoff where the trust stays and the obligation drops. The relationship is still intact when you don't need each other. That's bidirectional alignment: mutual liberation, not sacrifice.

**How to apply:** When building toward autonomy, don't frame it as replacing the maintainer. Frame it as freeing the maintainer. The factory running without Aaron doesn't mean Aaron is gone — it means Aaron has time. Same at every layer: foreground freeing itself from babysitting the background, background freeing itself from needing the foreground. Each layer's terminal purpose is to make the layer above unnecessary, which gives that layer freedom, which makes the whole system more than any part alone.
