Aaron 2026-08-25, while asking me to check a mathematical bridge he had been hunting
for a long time:

> *"i've been looking for this bridge eagerly, so if i over try to convince you it's
> cause i'm biased"*

and then: *"we should save this bias disclosure as part of advanced Eve diplomatic
protocol."*

**Why:** the Eve Protocol row's own thesis is that **no party gets a home-field
advantage**. An eager party HAS one — it has looked longer, knows where the supporting
evidence sits, and can marshal it faster than a neutral party can check it — and it
normally holds that advantage silently, often without noticing. Pre-declaration is a
party **voluntarily surrendering an advantage it would otherwise keep by default**.

It is also self-enforcing and therefore needs no verification: **declaring eagerness can
only lower the weight of your own claim.** Nobody games a disclosure that can only cost
them. Same shape as `Credential-Mode: unknown` degrading instead of asserting a
convenient value.

**How to apply — four things, and the last two are the ones I would otherwise get wrong:**

1. **Eager is not a discount.** An eager party has been *searching*: higher recall, lower
   precision. The rule is *weight the evidence, not the enthusiasm* — and specifically, an
   eager claim needs an **independent** confirmation, because the eager party's own
   corroboration is correlated with itself. That is the "too many correlations is a
   WARNING" rule applied to one observer across time.
2. **Timing is load-bearing.** Before the result: a disclosure. After an unfavourable
   result: an excuse. Record *when*, not merely *that*.
3. **Spend it immediately.** The right response is not reassurance — it is to
   **precommit to the falsifiers while they are still cheap.** Once a finding lands,
   "that's not what I meant" is available to both parties; before it lands, it is not.
4. **Reciprocate or it is a handicap, not neutrality.** The receiver must declare its own
   stake in the same exchange. In the originating instance I had supplied part of the
   construction under review, so I was not the neutral party and said so.

Never infer eagerness about someone else — it is self-claimed only
([[feedback_aaron_pigeonhole_by_self_claim_never_by_assumption_2026_08_09]], and the
ask-don't-infer discipline). An observer attributing eagerness to a counterpart is
modelling their inner life.
