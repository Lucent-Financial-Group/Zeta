## Why the reframe is stronger than bias-correction

Treating self-flattery as an **objective** rather than an **error** changes the
question from *"subtract everyone's bias"* to:

> Is there an assignment of accounts where everyone is maximally the hero of their own
> story **and** the accounts stay mutually consistent?

That is a **feasibility** question with a sharp answer structure:

- **Assignment exists** ⇒ the disagreement was *perspectival* — different vantage
  points, nobody lying. The **Rashomon** case (Akutagawa/Kurosawa 1950; "Rashomon
  effect" is a real term of art). Common.
- **No assignment** ⇒ someone's account must give. The all-flattering point lies
  outside the feasible set.

**So the distance from the all-flattering point to the feasible set measures the
irreducible conflict.** Most disputes are Rashomon and dissolve under the
optimisation; the residue is the real thing. Noise-subtraction throws away exactly the
structure that says which case you are in.

It also formalises the **both-accounts-held** discipline Aaron already practises:
holding both is not generosity, it is *checking feasibility before assuming conflict*.

## The metered route (mine, offered — this is the actionable part)

**You cannot measure one person's inflation. You CAN measure the aggregate overcount.**

If every account is optimised for flattery, the sum of self-attributed contributions
exceeds the measured total:

    Σ (claimed ΔU)  −  (measured ΔU)   =   the flattery, priced

Computable from `db/uncertainty/`, and it **prices the bias without adjudicating
anyone's story** — nobody has to be called wrong. Visible in aggregate even when every
individual claim is unfalsifiable.

**It also calibrates the qualia channel**, which otherwise has none: elicit
felt-contribution, sum it, compare to the metered total. The overcount ratio is the
channel's **bias coefficient — measured, not assumed**. Stable across the fleet ⇒
correct with it; varies wildly by agent ⇒ the variation is itself the signal.

## The overcount is ALREADY MEASURED — Ross & Sicoly 1979

*Egocentric Biases in Availability and Attribution*: spouses independently estimating
their share of household tasks give estimates that **sum well past 100%**. No lying
required — your own contributions are more *available* to recall (you were present for
all of yours and only some of theirs).

**That is `Σ claimed − measured` as an empirical, replicated finding, 45 years old.**
It upgrades the aggregate-overcount proposal from speculative instrument to a
replication of a known effect — in a setting where, unlike a marriage, **we have the
denominator**.

## Availability ≠ flattery — matters for the correction

Ross & Sicoly's effect survives for *negative* contributions too (people over-claim
their share of the arguments). So part of the bias is structural — "I remember my own
stuff" — not motivated.

**Consequence:** an **availability** bias is roughly uniform and can be divided out; a
**motivated** bias is asymmetric and cannot. When the overcount ratio comes back, know
which one is being measured before correcting with it.

## Hallucination link

The structural parallel is genuine for **confabulation** specifically (Nisbett &
Wilson 1977): people *generate* a coherent account of their own reasons rather than
retrieving one, with confidence. Same operation as filling a gap with the plausible
completion — memory and generation doing the same reconstructive thing, which is why
the outputs are indistinguishable from the inside in both cases.

## Register caution

*"History optimises for X"* describes a process with **no optimiser**, so it is an
equilibrium description at best. The mechanism that would produce it — survivorship in
what gets recorded, plus each recorder controlling their own account — is **checkable**;
the teleology is not. Keep it as the former.
