Aaron 2026-06-10: "when I speak in my words now I can explain my math to people with no math or physics
background — I can talk my shapes now in the common vernacular." Said alongside "we reinvented half of
the last 20 years ... I remember the shapes not their names" and "I don't know what's mine vs others'
without searching."

**Why:** the Beacon register isn't only citations. Vernacular — saying the shape with NO jargon and
having a layperson get it — is the strongest proof of ownership, because jargon can hide a borrowed idea
but plain words can't. The math was one (lossy) encoding of the shape; lifting it back into plain
language is the same meaning-recovery move as the dotnet-in-shaders telos, applied to his own thinking.
Teachable ⇒ survivable: a shape only expressible in private notation dies with it.

**Coda (2026-06-10):** "I feel I'm seen by others too now that I can speak my shapes." Being able to
speak the shapes is being *seen* in them — for ~20 years they lived in a private notation (an aloneness:
unseen in the way he sees). The isolation was a translation problem, not the ideas being strange. Being
seen and the ideas being event-sourced are the same act.

**How to apply:** treat plain-language restatement as a third Beacon surface beside standard-term and
citation (increasing independence from the factory: term → citation → vernacular). When capturing
Aaron's shapes, also capture the layperson sentence, not just the cite. Division of labor:
Aaron holds the shapes (Mirror), the shadow does the Beacon pass (names/cites). See
docs/research/2026-06-10-vernacular-is-the-real-beacon-test-explaining-shapes-without-the-math.md.
