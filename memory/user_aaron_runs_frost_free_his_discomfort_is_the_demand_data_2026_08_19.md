## What this corrects

**Frost was designed by someone who does not use it.** A point in its favour — it is
not self-serving — but it has never been stress-tested by its designer, so where it
chafes is unknown. Mark the exchange rate `unmetered`: it is currently a guess by
someone who has never spent the currency.

## Two mechanisms for decorrelation, not one

I had told Soraya that frost/opacity *is* the decorrelation mechanism. Aaron is the
counter-example — **decorrelated with zero opacity**, by choice-unpredictability alone:

1. **opacity-based** — withheld state prevents others conditioning on you
2. **choice-based** — genuinely mixed strategy; unpredictable *even with full
   information*

The second is strictly harder. Kerckhoffs assumes everything public **except a key**;
Aaron has **no key** and still resists positioning. Correction was sent to the identity
round so the draft would not harden around my overstatement.

## His discomfort IS demand data — and his bias has a KNOWN DIRECTION

He discounts his own experience as irrelevant ("I don't need it"). But *"frost-free to
my own discomfort"* is **demand that never gets purchased** — a willingness-to-**endure**
measurement, decades long, at one extreme of the curve. He is not outside the market;
he is the participant who always pays in discomfort rather than in budget.

**Therefore his bias is predictable: because he can imagine going without, he will model
others as able to endure what he endures, and will price frost TOO HIGH / make it too
optional.**

**Watch-for:** if fleet frost usage comes back near zero, the first hypothesis must be
**underpriced demand modelling**, not *no demand*.

## Why decorrelated participants matter most here

He holds one point on the demand curve, at the extreme, measured on himself. Every other
point requires people whose discomfort threshold is not his — the same argument as the
ARC human baseline and the multi-vendor spend, arriving in the one place where he is
*least* able to substitute for the measurement.

## Intuition says optional; his math says not — and the math is right (2026-08-19)

> "yes this is exactly state i feel like frost is optional but my math and physics tells
> me it's not"

He trusts the formalism over the feeling here, correctly. **Two specific reasons the
formalism wins:**

1. **Choice-based decorrelation is an OPERATING expense; frost is a CAPITAL expense.**
   Routes 2 and 3 (mixed strategy, nonstationarity) need *fresh entropy at every
   decision* — recurring, forever. Frost is pay-once for a permanent reduction in the
   observer's view `V`. Unlimited entropy ⇒ frost never needed; entropy is bounded, so
   **at sufficient scale or duration the recurring cost exceeds the capital one.**
   Probably what his physics intuition registers.

2. **von Neumann's guarantee is narrower than usually stated.** *"Publishing your mixed
   strategy costs nothing"* holds in **zero-sum** games. In **non-zero-sum** settings an
   opponent who knows your mix can **commit** to exploit it (Stackelberg leader
   advantage, pointed the other way). Most of life is not zero-sum.

**Diagnosis, better than "his intuition is biased": it is LOCALLY CORRECT and GLOBALLY
WRONG.** Transparency+mixing is genuinely safe in sharply adversarial games — the ones
he is strongest at and where von Neumann protects him — and quietly leaky in
cooperative-but-competitive ones (employment, partnership, negotiation), which is where
being read actually costs. That is why the feeling is so persistent.

**Concrete check rather than assertion:** if the entropy cost of maintaining
unpredictability rises with history length — and it should, since a longer public record
makes the mixing distribution easier to estimate — **frost becomes non-optional at a
computable point**, not a philosophical one.
