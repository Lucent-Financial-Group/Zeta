Aaron 2026-08-19, immediately after arguing the commercial bet is **meaning**:

> "and i love leaderboards cause i always game them to get to the top"

## Read it as a capability, not a confession

Someone who reflexively games leaderboards is the **ideal adversary to design
against**, and he has the instinct natively where most designers must imagine it. So
the correct posture here is unusually available: **assume the designer is the
attacker** — in this case he demonstrably is.

**Gaming a leaderboard is finding the metric's KERNEL** — where the number and the
intent diverge. That is exactly what the meter needs pointed at it, and it is already
the repo's discipline in another domain: `src/Core.TypeScript/hygiene/mutation-runner.ts`
(a test that survives mutation is not a falsifier).

> **His leaderboard instinct IS mutation testing for metrics.** Game the meter, publish
> the exploit, close it, repeat. It converts the thing he enjoys into the falsifier
> generator the meter cannot otherwise get.

## The tension to keep visible (do not "resolve" it)

He argues the bet is **meaning**, which is **non-positional**. He *enjoys*
**leaderboards**, which are purely **positional**. Not hypocrisy — a real pull, and it
will surface in the design, because people build for what motivates them.

**Specific risk: he will be tempted to build the leaderboard BECAUSE IT IS FUN, and the
leaderboard is the failure mode he would be building against**
([[tsmc-precision-bet-honest-meter-is-the-thesis]] — status is positional, meaning is
not; a meter consumed as ranking delivers competition instead of competence-feedback).

**Cheapest guard, offered and accepted-as-useful:** when he catches himself designing
the ranking view, ask which bet it serves. If the answer is *"it's satisfying,"* that is
the tell — and it is the moment the gaming instinct is most valuable pointed **inward**.
