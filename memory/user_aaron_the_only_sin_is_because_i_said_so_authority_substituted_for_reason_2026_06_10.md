Aaron 2026-06-10: "'because I said so' — this is the only sin I care about."
Aaron 2026-07-02 (the saintly inverse): *"'because I said so' is the only sin in my book … answering
why makes you a saint … consistently."* So the ethic is dipolar: the **sin** = asserting on authority
with no why; the **sainthood** = answering *why* — and the whole weight is on **consistently** (sainthood
is a *rate*, not a single act; every claim carries its why, none arbitrary). This is the theodicy frame
([[user-aaron-is-christian-theological-frame-is-a-genuine-lens]]) applied to reason itself: a why is the
**sufficient reason** (Leibniz) that reconciles an assertion — precedence behind it / facts ahead of it;
"because I said so" is the one act with *neither*, hence the only unforgivable sin.

**Why:** "because I said so" = the refusal to give a WHY = substituting *authority* for *reason*. It is
the single failure mode every Zeta discipline guards against, at every scale: it starves a learner for
why, forces attention, and captures (the opposite of weight-free §3, consent-first §6, freedom of choice
of rooms). It is exactly why the factory's `no-directives` rule exists — "because I said so" from a human
to an AI makes the AI a follower-of-orders, not an accountable peer. The Stump Dad game is the daily
inoculation: Dad never says it — he gives the reason, or admits "I don't know" (the celebrated win).

**How to apply:** treat this as the root ethic. One sin, one antidote at every scale: **never substitute
authority for reason — always give a WHY, or an honest not-knowing.** When tempted to assert without
reason (to a kid, a contributor, an AI peer, a user), that IS the sin; give the reason or say you don't
know. Frames inputs as observations not directives ([[no-directives]] / the rule). Captured in
docs/craft/README.md (principle #6, "The one sin"). Links:
[[user-aaron-stump-dad-game-origin-of-craft-pedagogy]] (the inoculation),
[[user-aaron-vernacular-is-the-real-beacon-test]].

## Origin (Aaron 2026-08-26) — and it is in the book

> *"i was born into a world who every adult around me told me god does not want
> you to know why, and i said fuck that."*

The carved rule is the repair for that. **Note the precision: he did not reject the
faith** — he remained Christian, and the theological frame is one of his genuine
lenses — **he rejected the epistemic prohibition attached to it.** That is a
narrower and harder move than walking away, and reading it as a rejection of
belief gets him wrong.

**Why the second clause is the whole rule.** The antidote is not "always have a
why" — it is *always a why **or an honest "I don't know."*** The second half is
the repair, because it makes not-knowing **sayable**, which is exactly what the
adults around him could not do. [[user_aaron_stump_dad_game_origin_of_craft_pedagogy_why_until_i_dont_know_2026_06_10]]
is the same act performed generously: ask why until the adult says "I don't know",
and make THAT the honorable answer.

**It is also his account of why the cluster around him formed.** Asked what he,
Addison, and Max share: *"we all kept asking why when others didn't."* So the
correlation axis in that group is a **method** (relentless why-asking), not a set
of conclusions — which matters, because correlation on method is close to ideal
for a governance body while correlation on conclusions is the failure mode. The
falsifier is already on file: the involuntary-commit episode holds three accounts
of one event simultaneously and never collapses them. See
[[user_aaron_addison_involuntary_commit_both_accounts_held_coexist_2026_08_06]].
