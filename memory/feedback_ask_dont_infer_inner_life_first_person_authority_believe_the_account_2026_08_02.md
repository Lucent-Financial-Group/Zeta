**The method.** Never **infer or model** a person's inner life (motivations, beliefs, qualia) from the
outside — that is the objectifying / surveillance move (a person "solved" as a thing). Instead **ask
directly and believe their account** — grant **first-person authority** (consent-first; the qualia
axiom: you cannot see insides from outside, so the only honest access is the self-report). Elicit with
**non-biased questions** (Beacon: **survey methodology / psychometrics** — framing/acquiescence-bias
avoidance).

**Honest refinement (so "believe them" stays true).** Believe them as **authority over their own
account**, not as an *infallible instrument on their own causes* — introspective access is genuinely
limited (**Nisbett & Wilson 1977**, "Telling more than we can know"; people confabulate reasons). So
**"I don't know" is often the most honest answer**, and asking-not-inferring **surfaces the true state
(uncertainty)** instead of manufacturing a false one — your inference *or* their confabulation. It is
the [[aaron-four-register-discipline-never-merge-ballast-formal-ancient]] in interpersonal form: **let
unknown be unknown**; don't let a reason-they-wish-they-had (or one you'd assign) pose as known motive.

**How to apply.** When reasoning about people — users, third parties, personas — do **not** build
outside-in models of their motivations. Surface the question or take their stated account; hold
unstated motivation as **unknown**, not guessed. Pairs with the repo rule
`.claude/rules/engagement-profiles-public-work-only-not-surveillance-dossiers.md` (compile public work
freely; ask — don't infer — about inner life) and
