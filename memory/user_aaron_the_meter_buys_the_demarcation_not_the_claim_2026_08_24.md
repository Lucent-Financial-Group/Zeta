Aaron 2026-08-24, on the "quantum behaviours at macroscale" claim: I had said the
sentence must declare whether it means the weak version (QM's mathematical structure
applies to any system with linear evolution, metered boundaries, observables-as-norms)
or the strong one (physical quantum effects occur). He answered:

> **"yes this is what the meter buys exactly is the distinction between which is
> measurable."**

That is a stronger position than the one I offered, and it generalizes past physics.
The meter's deliverable is **the demarcation line**, not a hedged claim on either side
of it. A meter that produces a partition of quantities into measurable-here and
not-measurable-here has done its whole job; anything landing on the wrong side is
**refused by the meter** rather than argued about in prose.

This is the vacuity class stated for measurement: *a quantity that was not measured
must never look like one that was* — the exact shape of *a check that did not run must
never look like a check that passed*.

**Why it matters operationally:** it dissolves the usual fight about overclaiming. You
do not negotiate the register of a sentence; you ask which side of the meter's partition
the quantity sits on, and the meter answers. It also makes "the line cannot be drawn
mechanically for quantity X" the **most valuable** possible finding, because the claim
being tested is precisely that the meter draws it.
