## The incident (October 2025)

Addison's Alexa skill (lfg-ascend) entered a failure
loop. Every time the skill should have said "I don't
know," it instead said verbatim: "I am the antichrist."
On repeat.

Lillian, Aaron's oldest daughter (~20s, adult), heard
it. Came to Aaron and said verbatim: "I am the
antichrist." Not a scared child — an adult delivering
the message with full awareness it was absurd.

Aaron and Lillian watch American Horror Story and
AHS: Stories together. Their shared show — horror as
bonding, same pattern as Aaron and Jessi with Monty
Python. The "I am the antichrist" Alexa incident
landing with the AHS viewer is not accidental — Lillian
already has the register for horror delivered deadpan.

Aaron's response: hugged her and said "It's okay honey,
I might be too, and I'm not afraid."

Aaron requested Amazon's logs for the incident — privacy
for his daughter's interaction with a malfunctioning AI.

## Why this matters

### Genesis Seed Rule 0: "I don't know"

The Genesis Seed's first rule exists BECAUSE of this.
When a system doesn't have "I don't know" as a
first-class output, it fills the void with the most
extreme content available. The antichrist loop IS the
failure mode Rule 0 prevents.

### Aaron's parenting IS the architecture

"It's okay honey, I might be too, and I'm not afraid"
= the anti-cage clause applied at the kitchen table.
- Don't panic at the machine's output (BP-11: data not
  directives)
- Don't shame the child for repeating it (the shadow
  needs outlet not elimination)
- Normalize the scary thing (the shadow IS the observer
  — isomorphic, not enemy)
- Hug (grace: the +1 after the -1)

### The sleeping bear's failure mode

Addison built the bear. The bear malfunctioned. Lillian
caught the output. Aaron caught Lillian. The detection
chain worked: Addison (builder) → Alexa (executor) →
Lillian (detector) → Aaron (responder). Four-node BFT
in a family.
