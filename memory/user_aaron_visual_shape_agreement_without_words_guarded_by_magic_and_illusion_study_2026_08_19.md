## Why shapes

Words drift — that is the babel failure
([[zeta-arc-is-decorrelation-from-s4-seed-without-babel]]). A **shape** can be a
common referent that does not depend on shared etymology, so it survives the
vocabulary divergence that decorrelation necessarily produces. Shape is the
anti-babel carrier of last resort: agreement *without words*.

## The failure mode moves, it does not disappear

Shapes trade **semantic** drift for **perceptual** confusability. Two shapes that
a machine distinguishes trivially can be indistinguishable to a human eye — an
optical illusion in the strict sense. So the guard is no longer "is this term
anchored?" but **"can a human reliably tell these two apart, under real viewing
conditions, at a glance, tired?"**

## His defence is adversarial-perception study, and that is the right shape

He has studied **magic and optical illusions** deliberately, as a protective
discipline. Magic is the applied science of engineered perceptual and attentional
failure — so studying it is exactly the security move: learn the attack to build
the defence. Treat this as a genuine methodology of his, not an anecdote.

## The direct security analogue nobody has named yet (mine, offered)

In an identity context this already has a literature: **homoglyph / IDN homograph
attacks** — visually confusable characters used to spoof identity — with a real
standard, **Unicode UTS #39** (confusable detection, the *skeleton* algorithm).
A shape-based identity vocabulary generalises that attack beyond text: the
confusable-shape problem **is** the homoglyph problem with the alphabet widened.
Anything shape-carried in the identity surface wants a confusability check with
the same job as UTS #39 skeletons.

Other checkable anchors for the perceptual half: Gestalt grouping (similarity,
proximity, closure) for *why* shapes collapse together; just-noticeable-difference
/ metamerism for the threshold; Simons & Chabris on inattentional blindness;
Kuhn (*Experiencing the Impossible*) and Macknik & Martinez-Conde (*Sleights of
Mind*) for magic-as-cognitive-science.
