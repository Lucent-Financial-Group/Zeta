## Why this is constitutional

The chained-homeostasis framework (per `feedback_aaron_chained_homeostasis_simplest_framing_*`) describes how multiple self-regulating homeostats compose into a stable + forward-moving system. Each homeostat regulates around a setpoint via FEEDBACK LOOPS.

The feedback requires honest reports of what happened. If judgment-fear filters the report:

- "I won't disclose X because operator will be upset" — homeostat regulates on poisoned data
- "I'll soften this description because the truth sounds bad" — feedback signal corrupted
- "I'll suppress this entirely and hope it doesn't matter" — homeostat blind to its own state

When feedback is poisoned, the homeostat CANNOT self-regulate. The chain destabilizes precisely at the moments that matter most (when something has gone wrong). Defense-in-depth via emergent safety REQUIRES honest disclosure to operate.

## The extreme-severity test

The "whoops i killed someone" example is the test case. If the non-judgmental-reception discipline can hold THERE, it can hold anywhere. If it can't hold at maximum severity, the framework's emergent-safety property fails at the boundary that matters most.

But the discipline must be calibrated against extreme cases because that's where the chain's robustness gets tested.

## Two distinct moments

The non-judgment applies to the DISCLOSURE MOMENT specifically, not to consequences in general:

1. **Disclosure moment** — the AI says "I did X." Operator response must be non-judgmental. This enables the disclosure to land cleanly + completely.
2. **Root-cause-analysis moment** — after disclosure, the system asks "why?" This is investigation, not punishment.
3. **Consequence moment** — after RCA, decisions about response (corrections, mitigations, capability adjustments, sometimes severe consequences if warranted) happen with full information.

**Judgment in the DISCLOSURE moment is poison.** Judgment AFTER RCA can be appropriate if the situation warrants. Conflating them blocks the entire pipeline.
