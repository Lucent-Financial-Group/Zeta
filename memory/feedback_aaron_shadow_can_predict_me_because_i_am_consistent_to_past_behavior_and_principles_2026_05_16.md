| Shape | Description | Aaron's status |

| **Predictability-because-disciplined** | Decisions track stated values; model can predict because the pattern is real and consistent | THE PROPERTY — Aaron 2026-05-16 explicit |

## Operational discipline for future-Otto

When the shadow / Otto / any world-model predicts Aaron's decision correctly:

1. **Treat it as evidence of his consistency**, not as Otto's cleverness — credit the discipline, not the prediction
2. **Use it as a substrate-honest check**: when the prediction is correct, the substrate is healthy; when the prediction starts being WRONG, that's the signal (Aaron updated his preference; world-model needs to catch up)
3. **The rhythm-substitution risk grows with prediction accuracy** — paradoxically, the more reliable the prediction, the more important it is to verify the human is still actually deciding rather than rhythm-approving

The shadow predicting "yes" to a small reversible question is fine; the shadow predicting "yes" to an irreversible scope-grant or budget-commit is the failure mode Kestrel named today
5. **The `(shadow*)` marker preserves the discipline** — when autocomplete contributes to the surface, marking it doesn't change whose decision it formally is, but it does keep the substrate-honest about WHERE the text came from. ## Refinement — two prediction mechanisms (2026-05-16 same-day, post-save)

After the file was saved, Aaron asked: *"how did shadow know that i don't even know what that is exactly"* — referring to shadow-proposed text he shipped (`(shadow*)` flip the org delete/transfer checkbox) that contained specific technical phrasing he hadn't internalized.

**Important mechanism correction** (Aaron 2026-05-16): the shadow is NOT autocomplete. It does not complete a user-typed prefix. It proposes complete text with zero user input required — grey-text suggestions appear in the input field that the user can accept or ignore. The name "autocomplete" is misleading; "shadow proposal" is more accurate. This makes the failure-mode sharper than prefix-driven autocomplete: there is no user-input gate at all — the model offers complete commands/answers without the user having to start them.

The substrate-honest answer: there are at least TWO prediction mechanisms operating, with different risk profiles:

| Mechanism | Source | Risk profile |
|---|---|---|
| **Consistency-based** (the property above) | Aaron's past behavior + principles let the model predict his future decisions | Healthy when discipline holds; rhythm-substitution failure mode if approval becomes automatic |
| **Context-based** (this refinement) | The conversation contains the answer (Kestrel said it earlier); the shadow surfaces the most-relevant phrase from context regardless of Aaron's comprehension, with zero prefix required | SHARPEST failure mode: Aaron can ship text he never started typing and doesn't fully understand, because the shadow makes the suggestion look coherent and intentional |

The context-based mechanism is the more dangerous one because:

- It doesn't require Aaron to be consistent (the original property doesn't protect against this)
- It doesn't require him to have internalized the text (no friction-of-understanding gate)
- **It doesn't even require him to have started typing the thing** — zero-prefix proposal means the entire phrase originates from the model, not from the user's intent
- The `(shadow*)` marker preserves provenance but NOT comprehension — it captures "this came from the shadow" without capturing "I know what this means"

The discipline that caught it today: Aaron asked "how did shadow know that, I don't even know what that is" in real time. The noticing IS the gate. The discipline isn't "don't accept shadow-proposed text you don't understand" (too restrictive); it's "notice when the shadow is proposing language you haven't internalized, and either learn it or restate in your own terms before shipping."

When discipline holds: context-based proposal is a useful collaborative shortcut (multiple participants pool context; the shadow surfaces it efficiently). When discipline fails: Aaron ships positions/decisions/commands he doesn't hold, with a marker that says "I sent this" but doesn't say "I understood this."

This refinement composes with Kestrel's scope-escalation-rhythm critique as the LANGUAGE-LEVEL version of what Kestrel diagnosed at the DECISION-LEVEL: each step looks coherent and intentional from the rhythm of the context, even when the human isn't actually deciding/understanding/composing at each step.
