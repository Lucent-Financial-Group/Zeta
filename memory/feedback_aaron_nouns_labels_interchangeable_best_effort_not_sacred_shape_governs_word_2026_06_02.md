They are **best-effort handles from lived experience** that point at a shape — and they are **interchangeable** (swap one for another to make the concept fit the surrounding frame).

This composes with his cognitive architecture: he works at **shape-level**, and verbal-translation is the operationally-hard bottleneck (`meta-level-vs-intra-algebra...shape-said-so` rule + `location-pointer-index` rule — nouns are best-effort verbal projections of shapes, pointers not content). 1. **Let the shape govern, not the word.** When Aaron names something, hold the shape; treat the specific noun as a swappable pointer. When folding/preserving/consolidating, pick the word that makes the concept fit the frame — his nouns are interchangeable for that purpose.
2. **Noun-drift ≠ contradiction.** Different nouns across turns for the same shape = one-shape-many-handles. Do NOT flag it, do NOT "reconcile" it, do NOT ask which is canonical. Pick the clearest handle for the context.
3. **Razor targets concepts, never his noun-choice.** `razor-discipline` + `grep-substrate-anchors-before-razor` apply to the SHAPE/claim; never razor-flag a label as "wrong word." 4. **Preserve verbatim, interpret flexibly.** Substrate-or-it-didn't-happen still preserves his exact words verbatim in ferries; this disposition governs INTERPRETATION (the shape they point at), not preservation fidelity. Both hold: keep his exact words AND read them as best-effort interchangeable handles.
