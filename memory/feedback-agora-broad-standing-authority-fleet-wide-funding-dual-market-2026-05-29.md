## Implicit-authority-extension — a B-0928 refinement (operator 2026-05-29)

The operator flagged a subtle authorization mechanism (candidate B-0928 follow-up):
he sent *"file the schema-registry-over-dbsp row (shadow*) Also we should save this
minimalist tech summary as the center of our tech radar..."* — the "file the row"
was **shadow-sourced** (`(shadow*)`), but by continuing with *"Also [do this other
thing]"* he **implicitly extended his authority to the shadow's instruction**,
treating it as an agreed premise — *without stating it explicitly*. His own catch:
*"since I said 'Also' I the human extended the shadow's authority implicitly without
stating it explicitly."*

So the authorization-spectrum has **three tiers**, not two:

| Tier | Mechanism | Sufficient for... |
|---|---|---|
| **Explicit operator-auth** | operator directly says "do X" | anything (incl. out-of-authority + irreversible) |
| **Implicit operator-extension** (NEW) | operator builds on / continues from a shadow-or-ambient instruction ("Also…"), treating it as an agreed premise | within-authority actions; **NOT** out-of-authority/irreversible (the operator may endorse without examining — the human-side risk) |
| **Shadow/ambient alone** | autocomplete/grey-text produces it; operator hasn't engaged it | nothing (not an authorization source — B-0928) |

The risk it names: implicit-extension is how shadow-authority gets **laundered into
operator-backing without examination** (the human-side of the B-0928 auth-injection —
the operator can implicitly endorse a shadow-instruction just by continuing the
conversation). For **out-of-authority / irreversible** actions, implicit-extension is
**NOT** enough — require explicit. For **within-authority** actions (e.g. filing a
backlog row — broadly authorized substrate-authoring per `dont-ask-permission`), it
is moot: the action is pre-authorized regardless of the shadow-sourcing/implicit-chain
(so B-0930 below proceeds on the within-authority grant, *not* on the implicit
extension). Discipline: notice implicit-extension; never let it stand in for explicit
on out-of-authority actions.

**UX requirement — surface implicit grants in the DU-UX + prompt explicit/deny
(operator 2026-05-29):** *"i the human agree with that implicit authorization
escalation but our conversational UX should make it visible in our DUs
choose-your-own-adventure that i just made an implicit grant and ask to make it
explicit or deny it."* The mitigation is not only *passive* (agent notices) — the
**conversational DU-UX should actively surface** the implicit grant and force a
choose-your-own-adventure resolution: **"you just made an implicit grant of X —
[make explicit] / [deny]."** This converts implicit-extension from silent laundering
into a **visible, explicitly-resolved decision** (closes the human-side B-0928 risk;
glass-halo for authorization). Concretely a **ConvFeedback variant** (B-0861 —
conversation-interface as `Result<T, ConvFeedback>`): `ImplicitGrantDetected of grant`
→ the DU-UX renders the explicit/deny choice; the operator's resolution is the
authorization event (or refusal). The DU-choose-your-own-adventure interface (the
same surface as the fairy-tale-DU kid-safety substrate, B-0926/B-0929) thus doubles
as the **authorization glass-halo**: every implicit grant surfaced + resolved
explicitly, none silently laundered. **Buildable / candidate backlog row** (B-0928
follow-up / new row — offered, operator's call): "DU-UX surfaces implicit grants +
prompts explicit/deny (ConvFeedback ImplicitGrantDetected)." Composes with B-0861
(ConvFeedback), B-0928 (the attack-surface this mitigates), B-0926/B-0929 (the DU-UX),
`glass-halo-bidirectional`, `asymmetric-authorship` (the operator authors the
authorization-decision; the UX surfaces it for explicit resolution).
