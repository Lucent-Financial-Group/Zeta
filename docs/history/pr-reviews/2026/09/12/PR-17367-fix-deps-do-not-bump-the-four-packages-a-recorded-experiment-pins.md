## Description

#17343 moved `numpy 2.5.1 → 2.5.3` and turned `full-verify` red with `UPSTREAM_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. **That is the fixture doing its job, not a CI defect.**

`minigrid_empty_5x5_v310_fixture.py` replays a recorded MiniGrid trajectory and calls `validate_upstream_runtime`, which compares the **live** versions of python, minigrid, gymnasium, numpy and pygame-ce against the carrier committed at `docs/research/data/2026-09-06-minigrid-empty-5x5-v310-adapter-carrier.json`, then hashes three minigrid source files. Any difference raises.

The carrier records what the experiment **ran under**. A replay under other versions is a different experiment wearing the same name.

That is why exactly those four carry `==` in `pyproject.toml` while everything else carries `>=`. Dependabot was bumping an exact pin that exists to hold a replay — so the PR was red by construction, and would be again every week.

### The remedy is not to widen the pin or edit the carrier

Re-recording a dated provenance artifact so a patch bump can pass destroys the only evidence that the trajectory was ever reproduced. It converts a falsifiable record into a value that tracks whatever happens to be installed — the vacuity class, applied to research data.

So the four are ignored for the `uv` ecosystem, with the reason stated inline and a **LIFTS WHEN**: someone re-runs the adapter under new upstream versions, confirms the trajectory is unchanged (or records that it changed, and why), and commits a **new dated carrier beside the old one**. That is a research act with an artifact, not a dependency update, and it does not belong to a bot.

Written in the house style of the `dotnet-sdk` entry directly below it, which already says of its own PRs that they *"will be RED, by construction, and that is the design rather than a defect."*
