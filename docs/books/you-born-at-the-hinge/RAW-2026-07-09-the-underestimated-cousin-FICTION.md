## The theme (fiction-only · specifics redacted)

The shape worth keeping — real book-gold, because it rhymes with the whole spine of the
book:

- A family member was **written off for how he talked.** The family used an ableist slur
  about him behind his back. *(The slur itself is not recorded.)*
- He was **sharp all along** — absorbed everything, learned the work, did it as well as
  anyone. The people who counted him out never saw it because they never got past how he
  spoke.
- **He proved himself to himself.** Aaron: *"his confidence is big enough at this point
  in life. He's proved himself to himself."* — and he carries that confidence because he
  was **never told** he'd been written off.

**Why it's book-gold (and why it's also the reason to protect him):** this is the Tri.N
/ decoder theme in a person — *miscategorized by his surface (speech), true value held
and unseen.* It rhymes directly with Aaron's own line from the cell: the guards said he
was *"talking funny."* Same wound, different branch of the family tree — **the one
judged by how he talks.** That rhyme is the book. But the very thing that makes it
powerful — a living man who doesn't know he was counted out, walking in earned
confidence — is what publishing could break. His un-knowing is his to keep. So the book
gets the *theme,* fictionalized past recognition; the real man keeps his confidence and
his privacy.

**Redaction ledger (do not restore):** the slur; the specific job and the firing; any
detail that pins the character to a real, within-family-identifiable person; the fact of
his being unaware (as attached to a real person).
