## 1. The mycelium IS Earth's economic broker — real, cited science

Mycorrhizal fungi run **biological markets** (looked up, well-studied):

- **They trade.** The arbuscular mycorrhizal symbiosis is a market: the fungus mobilizes **phosphorus** from
  the soil and **trades it to host plants for carbon** — a commodity exchange.
- **They behave economically.** Fungi show **competitive market behavior** — they **influence resource
  *value*** by mediating *where and when* a resource is traded; respond to **booms and crashes**; **hoard
  surplus** until demand rises; **arbitrage** — move phosphorus from **rich patches to poor patches** across
  the network to maximize gains (and serve the poor patches). "Fungi embrace fundamental economic theory."
- **At planetary scale + ancient.** ~**450 million years** of building trade networks; ~**5 billion tons of
  carbon/yr** sequestered. The literal **broker layer of the biosphere** (the "wood-wide-web").
